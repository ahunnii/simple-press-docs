## Overview

Using SimplePress means agreeing to a few written documents. This page sums them up in plain words: who agrees to what, what you're responsible for, what SimplePress does and doesn't promise, and what happens if something goes wrong.

<Callout type="warn" title="This is a summary, not legal advice">
  This page is here to help you understand the documents. It is not legal advice, and it doesn't replace them. If this page and a document ever disagree, **the document wins**. Read the full text before you rely on any part of it, and talk to a lawyer if you have legal questions.
</Callout>

SimplePress is run by **The Center for Generative Justice LLC**. When the documents say "SimplePress", "we" or "us", that's who they mean.

## Who agrees to what, and when

There are two separate agreements. One goes with your **account**. The other goes with each **store** you own.

| Who | What they agree to | When |
| --- | ------------------ | ---- |
| Anyone who creates a SimplePress account with an email address, including Owners, Managers, Staff and customers | **Terms of Service** and **Privacy Policy** | At sign-up. The form has a required box: "I agree to SimplePress's Terms of Service and Privacy Policy." An account can't be created without it. |
| Every **Owner** of a store | **Seller & Merchant Agreement** and **Acceptable Use Policy** | When they set up or claim the store. Owners who join another way see a one-time terms screen the first time they open the admin. |

The one-time screen is titled **One thing before you continue**. The Owner ticks the box and clicks **Agree and continue**. If they've never accepted the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy, the same box covers those too. Managers and Staff never see it. See [Owners see a terms screen once](/docs/team-and-roles#owners-see-a-terms-screen-once).

Some things to know:

- **The store agreement is per store.** If you own two stores, you accept it for each one.
- **SimplePress records when you agreed** and which version of the documents you agreed to.
- **You won't be asked again when a document changes.** See [Changes to the terms and fees](#changes-to-the-terms-and-fees).

## What SimplePress is, and isn't

SimplePress is software and hosting that lets you run your own online store. Usually we build your business its own custom template, and you'll need your own custom domain before you can sell.

SimplePress is **not**:

- the seller of your products, or the "merchant of record"
- a payment processor. Card payments go through **Stripe**, straight to your own Stripe account. SimplePress never holds your money.
- a shipping or fulfillment company
- your partner, agent or employer

Your customers buy **from you**, not from SimplePress. The Terms of Service tell customers that problems with products, refunds or delivery have to be sorted out with the business they bought from.

Help from SimplePress, like building your template, setting up your store or answering questions, doesn't change this. It doesn't make SimplePress your partner or agent.

## What you can't sell or do

The Acceptable Use Policy has the full list, and it says that not everything can be listed. SimplePress decides whether something breaks the rules. In short, you can't use SimplePress to sell, promote or do any of these:

- **Illegal or dangerous goods:** illegal products or services, stolen or counterfeit goods, controlled substances and drug paraphernalia, illegal weapons, firearms or ammunition where prohibited, explosives or hazardous materials.
- **Adult or exploitative content:** pornography or sexually explicit material, child sexual abuse material, non-consensual intimate images, sexual deepfakes, and escort or prostitution services.
- **Harmful content:** hate speech, extremist propaganda, threats, harassment, or content that promotes self-harm or violence.
- **Scams and false claims:** scams and pyramid schemes, fake reviews or testimonials, false medical or financial claims, miracle cures, and misleading listings.
- **Gambling and risky finance:** illegal gambling, unlicensed financial services or investment schemes, unregistered securities, and money laundering.
- **Digital abuse:** malware, spyware, phishing kits and hacking tools.
- **Spam and abuse of the platform:** spam or unlawful marketing emails, fake accounts, review manipulation, scraping, or trying to get around SimplePress's security.

If these rules are broken, SimplePress can remove content, turn off products, or suspend or close a store or account. This can happen **with or without notice**. Repeat offenders can be removed for good. See [If Your Store Is Paused or Closed](/docs/leaving-or-closing).

## Your content is yours

- **You own what you upload.** Your product listings, photos, logos, text and designs stay yours.
- **You give SimplePress permission to use it to run the platform.** That means storing it, showing it on your store, making copies and resizing it. This permission is only for operating, improving and securing SimplePress.
- **SimplePress is open source.** Its code, including the website templates, is released under the AGPL-3.0 license at [github.com/ahunnii/simple-press](https://github.com/ahunnii/simple-press). You can use, change and share that code under the license's terms. What the license doesn't cover: the SimplePress and Center for Generative Justice names and logos, and access to the hosted service. You can't present your own copy as SimplePress or resell access to ours without written permission.
- **When you leave, your content goes with you.** [Download Your Site](/docs/data-and-export) gives you a full copy, with a **Licenses** folder that explains who owns what.

## Copyright complaints (DMCA)

**If someone copies your work** on a SimplePress store, send a written DMCA notice to SimplePress's DMCA Agent. The contact details, and the list of what the notice must include, are in the [DMCA Policy](https://simplepress.dev/platform/policies/dmca). You'll need to include, for example, the address of the copied material, a statement made in good faith, and your signature.

**If someone complains about content on your store**, SimplePress can remove it or hide it, and may tell you. It can also remove content that looks like it infringes even without a formal notice.

**If you think your content was removed by mistake**, you can send a counter-notice. The DMCA Policy lists what it must include. If it's valid, SimplePress may put the content back, unless the person who complained goes to court within the time the law allows.

Stores that infringe again and again can be suspended or closed. Sending a notice you know is false can make you legally liable.

## INFORM Act

A federal law, the INFORM Consumers Act, requires identity checks for **high-volume sellers**: 200 or more sales, or $5,000 or more in revenue, in a 12-month period.

SimplePress uses **Stripe's identity verification** for this. Your Finances page tracks both numbers and prompts you to finish verifying with Stripe when you cross either one. SimplePress doesn't turn off your payments if you don't, but you're expected to complete it. See [INFORM Act compliance](/docs/finances#inform-act-compliance).

## What SimplePress doesn't promise

SimplePress is free, still growing, and run on a best-effort basis. The documents say:

- **No guaranteed uptime.** The platform may go down, and features may change, break or be removed.
- **No guarantee your data is kept.** That's why backups are your job.
- **It's provided "as is".** There's no promise of sales, business success or error-free operation.
- **Liability is capped.** SimplePress's total liability is limited to $100 USD, and it isn't liable for things like lost sales, lost data, payment failures or outages.
- **You cover claims caused by your store.** If someone makes a claim against SimplePress because of your products, content or actions, you agree to cover it.

For what you *can* expect from our team day to day, see [Expectations](/docs/expectations).

## Disagreements with SimplePress

Under the Terms of Service and the Seller & Merchant Agreement, a disagreement with SimplePress first goes to informal, good-faith talks for 30 days. If that doesn't settle it, it goes to **binding arbitration** in Washtenaw County, Michigan, under American Arbitration Association rules. You give up the right to sue in court, to a jury trial, and to take part in a class action. **Michigan law** applies.

## Changes to the terms and fees

**The documents can change.** The Seller & Merchant Agreement, Acceptable Use Policy and DMCA Policy each say a new version takes effect **as soon as it's posted**, unless it says otherwise. Continuing to use SimplePress counts as accepting it. Check the **Last Updated** dates on the [Legal & Policies page](https://simplepress.dev/platform/policies) from time to time.

**Fees today:** SimplePress doesn't charge a platform fee or take a cut of your sales. Stripe still charges its own processing fees. See [Payments and Payouts](/docs/payments-and-payouts).

**Fees later:** both the Terms of Service and the Seller & Merchant Agreement keep the right to add fees, subscription plans or paid features, or to limit free use, in the future. They say **reasonable notice will generally be given** before a major pricing change takes effect.

**Emails from SimplePress:** by using SimplePress you agree to get emails about your account, security, moderation, legal notices and platform updates.
