## Overview

Analytics shows you how people use your public storefront. You can see how many visitors came, which pages and products they looked at, where they came from, how far they got toward buying, and how many orders and leads you got.

The numbers come from Umami, a privacy-friendly visitor counter that SimplePress runs for you. It doesn't use cookies and doesn't load any outside script in your visitors' browsers. You can also add your own Google Analytics and Meta Pixel on **Settings → Integrations**. If you do, your visitors see a [cookie banner](#cookie-banner-and-your-visitors).

It's useful when you want to answer questions like:

- Did my newsletter or social post actually send people to the store?
- Which products and pages get the most attention?
- Are people adding items to the cart but not checking out?
- How many sign-ups, leads and donations came in this month?

<Callout type="info" title="Off by default, and two things must be on">

Analytics is **off by default**. You need to turn on **two** things:

1. The **Analytics** switch under **Settings → Features**. This adds the **Analytics** page to your admin.
2. The **Collect analytics** switch in the **Umami Analytics** card under **Settings → Integrations**. This is what actually starts counting visitors.

Only Owners and Managers can open this page and change these settings. Staff can't. See [Team and Roles](/docs/team-and-roles) and [Feature Flags](/docs/feature-flags).

</Callout>

## Turning It On

### Step 1: Turn on the Analytics switch

1. Go to **Settings → Features**.
2. Find **Analytics** in the Marketing section and switch it on.

Once it's on, **Analytics** appears in your admin menu. If you open the page before turning the switch on, you'll see **Analytics is not enabled for this business** with an **Enable Analytics** button that takes you to **Settings → Features**.

![The Analytics is not enabled for this business page with an Enable Analytics button](/images/analytics/analytics-feature-off.png)

### Step 2: Switch on Collect analytics

1. Go to **Settings → Integrations**.
2. In the **Analytics & tracking** section, find the **Umami Analytics** card.
3. Switch on **Collect analytics**.

That's it. The switch saves the moment you flip it. SimplePress sets up your store's counter for you, so there's no Website ID to paste and nothing to email us about. The badge in the card's top right changes from **Off** to **On**. You'll also see a message reminding you to update your Privacy Policy to mention Umami. See [Integrations](/docs/integrations#umami-analytics) for the full steps.

![The Umami Analytics card on Settings → Integrations with the Collect analytics switch on and the On badge showing](/images/integrations/umami-card.png)

<Callout type="info" title="Counting starts when you turn it on">

Visits are only counted from the moment **Collect analytics** is on. You won't see numbers for days before that. Give it a few days of normal traffic before drawing conclusions.

</Callout>

If the **Analytics** page says **Analytics is off**, **Collect analytics** isn't on yet. Use the **Go to Settings → Integrations** link on that page.

## Where to Find It

Go to **Analytics** in your admin menu. The page is titled **Analytics**, with the line "Who visits your store, where they come from, and what they do." The date picker and a **Download CSV** button sit in the top right.

## Choosing a Date Range

Use the picker in the top right to change the period. Everything on the page changes with it.

| Choice | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Last 24 hours** | The past day, with the chart broken out hour by hour |
| **Last 7 days** | The past week, with the chart broken out day by day |
| **Last 30 days** | The past month. This is what you see when you first open the page. |
| **Last 90 days** | The past three months, day by day |

The ranges always count back from right now. They are not calendar months, and you can't type in your own dates.

![The date picker open with Last 24 hours, Last 7 days, Last 30 days and Last 90 days, with a check next to the selected range](/images/analytics/analytics-range-picker.png)

## The Four Stat Cards

The row of cards at the top gives you the big picture.

| Card | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Visitors** | How many different people visited your store in this period. Someone who comes back three times still counts once. Underneath, "here now" shows how many people are on your site at this moment. |
| **Pageviews** | How many pages were opened in total. One visitor looking at five pages makes five pageviews. |
| **Bounce rate** | The share of visits where the person left after looking at only one page. Underneath it says "Visits that saw one page." |
| **Avg. time per visit** | How long a typical visit lasts, for example "2m 14s." |

Each card also shows how it changed compared with the period just before. For example, with **Last 30 days** selected, a line such as "12% vs the 30 days before" appears under the number, with a green arrow for a good change and red for a bad one. For **Bounce rate**, down is good, so a drop shows green. The arrow stays grey when there's no real change, or when the earlier period had fewer than 10 visitors or pageviews, since a big percentage from a tiny number doesn't mean much. If there's no earlier data yet, you'll see a dash instead.

A few things to know:

- A **bounce** isn't always bad. Someone who lands on your contact page, finds your phone number and leaves has "bounced," but they got what they came for.
- A **visit** is one trip to your site. The same person can make several visits.
- When there are no visits yet, **Bounce rate** and **Avg. time per visit** show a dash instead of a number.

## Visitors and Pageviews

Under the cards, the **Visitors and pageviews** chart shows traffic over time as two lines, one for **Pageviews** and one for **Visitors**. Hover over a point to see the exact numbers for that hour or day.

- On **Last 24 hours**, each point is one hour. On the other ranges, each point is one day.
- Hours and days follow your store's time zone, which is shown under the chart title.

If nothing was recorded in the period, you'll see "No visits in this period yet."

Spikes are worth a look. Think about what you did that day, like a post, an email or a sale, to see what worked.

![The Visitors and pageviews chart with the tooltip showing the pageviews and visitors for one day](/images/analytics/visitors-chart.png)

## How Shoppers Move Through Your Store

**How shoppers move through your store** lists four steps in order, each with a count and a bar:

| Step | What it counts |
| --- | --- |
| **Viewed a product** | Times someone opened a product page |
| **Added to cart** | Times someone added something to their cart |
| **Started checkout** | Times someone began checking out |
| **Finished checkout** | Times a purchase was completed |

Every step after the first also shows how much of the step before it carried on, such as "40% of the step before." If many people view products but few add to cart, look at your product photos, prices and descriptions. If many add to cart but few start checkout, look at your shipping costs and cart page. If many start checkout but few finish, look at your payment and shipping options.

These count **actions**, not people. One visitor who adds three products to the cart makes three **Added to cart** actions. They come from tracking in the shopper's browser, so a few can be missed.

![The How shoppers move through your store card with Viewed a product, Added to cart, Started checkout and Finished checkout, each with a count and a bar](/images/analytics/shopper-steps.png)

## Sales

The **Sales** card is different from the rest of the page. It comes from your store's own orders, not from browser tracking, so the numbers are exact.

| Number | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Paid orders** | Orders that were paid in this period |
| **Revenue** | The total of those paid orders |
| **Visitors who ordered** | Paid online orders divided by visitors. Sales rung up on a register are left out. |

Your [Orders](/docs/orders) list is still the official record of every sale.

![The Sales card showing Paid orders, Revenue and Visitors who ordered](/images/analytics/sales-card.png)

## Sign-ups and Leads

**Sign-ups and leads** counts four things people do on your store:

- **Form, contact and quote requests**
- **Newsletter sign-ups**
- **Donations**
- **Subscriptions started**

These come from browser tracking, so a few can be missed. Your own lists (form responses, customers, donations) are the official record.

![The Sign-ups and leads card with Form, contact and quote requests, Newsletter sign-ups, Donations and Subscriptions started](/images/analytics/signups-card.png)

## The Tables

Tables sit below the cards. Each shows its top rows with a count, and says what's missing when it's empty, for example "No product views in this period."

| Table | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Top pages** | The pages opened most, shown as addresses such as `/` for your home page or `/shop` for your shop |
| **Where visitors come from** | Other websites that sent visitors, such as `google.com` or `instagram.com`, with **Visits** |
| **Most viewed products** | Products ranked by **Views** |
| **Devices** | What visitors used, such as desktop or mobile, with **Visitors** |
| **Countries** | Where in the world visitors were, with **Visitors** |

Visitors who typed your address, used a bookmark or tapped a link from an app that doesn't pass along where it came from aren't listed in **Where visitors come from**, because there's no website to show.

### Campaign links

**Campaign links** shows visits from links you've tagged with `utm_source` or `utm_campaign`. These are small labels added to the end of a link, like `?utm_source=newsletter&utm_campaign=spring-sale`. Put them on links in your emails, social posts and ads and you'll see which one sent people. There's a **Source** table and a **Campaign** table, each with **Visits**. If nobody has visited a tagged link, it says "No tagged links visited in this period."

<Callout type="info" title="Tip: tag your links">

Email apps often don't pass along where a click came from. Tagging your newsletter links with `utm_source` is the reliable way to see them here.

</Callout>

![The Top pages, Where visitors come from, Campaign links and Most viewed products tables, with the empty messages showing on the two that had no visits](/images/analytics/tables-top.png)

![The Devices and Countries tables, each with a Visitors column](/images/analytics/tables-devices.png)

## Embedded Widgets

If you use [Embeds](/docs/embeds), such as a booking form from another service, an **Embedded widgets** card appears. It only shows when the **Embeds** feature is on.

| Number | What it counts |
| --- | --- |
| **Click-ins** | Times a visitor clicked into an embedded widget |
| **Clicked back out** | Times a visitor clicked back out again. This is approximate, because switching browser tabs counts too, and visits over 30 minutes are left out. |

Browsers don't let your store see what happens inside someone else's widget, so these numbers only tell you that people interacted with it, not what they did there.

![The Embedded widgets card with Click-ins and Clicked back out](/images/analytics/embedded-widgets.png)

## Also See

If you've set up Google Analytics or the Meta Pixel, an **Also see** card links to **Open Google Analytics** and **Open Meta Events Manager**. Those open in a new tab. The card also explains why numbers differ between tools: SimplePress Analytics counts every visit without cookies, while Google and Meta only count visitors who allow them. See [Integrations](/docs/integrations#google-analytics).

If **Collect analytics** is off but you've set up Google Analytics or the Meta Pixel, the **Also see** card still appears under the **Analytics is off** message.

## Downloading the Numbers

Click **Download CSV** in the top right to save what's on the page as a spreadsheet file. It contains the date range you've picked: the overview with the previous period for comparison, the chart's days or hours, the shopping steps, sales, sign-ups and leads, and each table. The file is named like `analytics-30d-2026-11-17.csv`.

## Cookie Banner and Your Visitors

Umami doesn't use cookies, so a store with only Umami has no banner and nothing for visitors to click or accept.

Once you add **Google Analytics** or the **Meta Pixel** under **Settings → Integrations**, your storefront shows a cookie banner. Neither tool loads for a visitor until they allow it.

### When the banner appears

- It appears only on stores with Google Analytics or the Meta Pixel set up, and only until the visitor makes a choice.
- Google Analytics and the Meta Pixel never load for you or your team while you're signed in, or on admin, sign-in and account pages.
- If a visitor's browser sends a Global Privacy Control signal, advertising stays off automatically. A store with only the Meta Pixel won't ask that visitor anything.

### The choices

- With **one** tool set up, the banner has **Accept** and **Decline**.
- With **both**, it has **Accept all**, **Reject all** and **Choose**. **Choose** opens two switches, **Analytics** and **Advertising**, and a **Save** button. **Analytics** covers Google Analytics. **Advertising** covers the Meta Pixel and Google's advertising features.

The banner links to your Privacy Policy once you've published one. See [Pages and Policies](/docs/pages-and-policies#privacy-policy-warnings).

### Reopening preferences

A small link sits at the very bottom of every page, below your footer. When Google Analytics or the Meta Pixel is set up, it reads **Cookie preferences**, or **Your privacy choices** when the Meta Pixel is on. Visitors can use it any time to change their mind.

On a store whose only outside tool is the Chat assistant, there's no banner. The chat tool loads only when a visitor taps it. The bottom link reads **Privacy policy** and goes to your Privacy Policy page.

![The cookie banner in the bottom-left corner of a storefront page on a computer, reading "This store uses cookies for analytics and ads, only if you allow them." with a Privacy Policy link and Accept all, Reject all and Choose buttons](/images/analytics/cookie-banner-desktop.png)

![The banner after clicking Choose, with Analytics and Advertising switches, both off, and Accept all, Reject all and Save buttons](/images/analytics/cookie-banner-choices.png)

![The cookie banner across the bottom of a storefront page on a phone, with Accept all, Reject all and Choose buttons](/images/analytics/cookie-banner-phone.png)

Turning on any of these tools means your Privacy Policy needs to mention it. **Settings → Integrations** shows a notice when it doesn't, and the Policies page can insert updated sections for you to review. SimplePress can't give legal advice. If you want to be sure what your policy or local rules require, check with a qualified professional.

## Tips

- **Be patient.** Give it a few days of normal traffic before you read much into the numbers. Small stores can have very small numbers, and that's normal.
- **Your admin work isn't counted, but browsing your storefront can be.** Time spent in your admin, on sign-in pages, in the Site Editor (and its preview), and on invoice payment pages is never counted. Umami also skips you and your staff while you're signed in. If you browse your storefront in a private window or while signed out, those visits show up, so the numbers can look higher in a brand-new store.
- **Compare ranges.** Switch between **Last 7 days** and **Last 30 days** to tell a one-time spike from a real trend. The arrows under each card do this for you.
- **Tag your links.** Use `utm_source` and `utm_campaign` so **Campaign links** can tell you what worked.
- **Look at the shopping steps.** **How shoppers move through your store** shows where shoppers drop off.
- **Check Top pages for dead ends.** If a page you care about is missing, link to it from your menu or home page.
- **Need more detail than this page shows?** The full Umami dashboard is run by the SimplePress team, and store owners don't get a login. Email [help@generativejustice.org](mailto:help@generativejustice.org) if you need a number this page doesn't show.

## Feature Flag

| Feature | Default | Where to toggle | Requires |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Analytics** | Off | Settings → Features (Marketing) | Nothing |

You also need **Collect analytics** switched on in the **Umami Analytics** card under **Settings → Integrations**. The two settings do different jobs:

- **Collect analytics** decides whether visitors are **counted**.
- The **Analytics** feature switch decides whether you can **see** the page.

If you turn counting on but leave the feature switch off, visitors are still counted but you can't open the page yet. If you turn the feature on but not counting, the page shows **Analytics is off**. Turning the feature off hides the page, but doesn't erase anything already counted. See [Feature Flags](/docs/feature-flags).

You can also add **Conversion rate** and **Visitors** cards to your [Dashboard](/docs/dashboard#conversion-rate). They use the same visitor counts and appear only when the **Analytics** switch and **Collect analytics** are on and your store has recorded some visitors.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do my customers see anything about this?**
Not with Umami alone. It counts in the background with no cookies, so there's nothing for visitors to click or accept. If you've added Google Analytics or the Meta Pixel, visitors see a cookie banner and choose what to allow. See [Cookie banner and your visitors](#cookie-banner-and-your-visitors). Either way, keep your Privacy Policy up to date.

**The numbers look too low.**
Counting starts when you switch on **Collect analytics**. It can't count earlier visits. Some visitors also use browsers or blockers that stop all counters, so the real number is often a little higher than what you see. Google Analytics and the Meta Pixel will usually show lower numbers still, because they only count visitors who allow them.

**The numbers look too high.**
Visits to your admin, the sign-in pages, the Site Editor and its preview, and invoice payment pages are never counted, and Umami skips you and your team while you're signed in. But visits to your storefront from a signed-out browser or a private window are counted, which is most noticeable if you check your store a lot while setting it up.

**I turned everything on but still see "Analytics is off."**
Open **Settings → Integrations** and check that the **Umami Analytics** card badge says **On**. If not, switch on **Collect analytics**.

**Why might my sales numbers differ from my order count?**
The **Sales** card comes straight from your orders, so it matches. The shopping steps and sign-up counts come from browser tracking, and a few can be missed, for example if a visitor uses a blocker. Your [Orders](/docs/orders) list is the official record.

**Can I set a custom date range?**
No. The page shows fixed ranges (last 24 hours, 7, 30 or 90 days). You can click **Download CSV** to save the numbers for the range you picked.

## What You Can and Cannot Do

**You can:**

- See visitors, pageviews, bounce rate and average time per visit for the last 24 hours, 7 days, 30 days or 90 days, compared with the period before
- See a chart of visitors and pageviews over time
- See how shoppers move from viewing a product to finishing checkout
- See paid orders and revenue from your own orders
- See form, newsletter, donation and subscription counts
- See your top pages, most viewed products, where visitors came from, tagged campaign links, devices and countries
- See embed click-ins if you use Embeds
- Download the page as a CSV file
- Add Conversion rate and Visitors cards to your Dashboard

**You cannot:**

- See visits from before **Collect analytics** was turned on
- Pick your own start and end dates
- Filter the page by device, country or one product
- Open the full Umami dashboard yourself. Store owners don't get a login.
- Let Staff see this page. It is for Owners and Managers only.
