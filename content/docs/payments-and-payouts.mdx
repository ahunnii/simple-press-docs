## Overview

When a customer pays by card, the money goes straight to **your own Stripe account**. SimplePress never holds your funds, and it takes no fee. Stripe then sends the money on to your bank on a schedule you control in Stripe.

This page explains how that works:

- how a payment becomes a payout, and how long it takes;
- what you pay in fees;
- where to see your money;
- refunds, bank debits and other delayed payment methods, and other ways to get paid;
- what happens when Stripe pauses payments; and
- what to do when a customer disputes a charge.

You need a connected Stripe account to take card payments. See [Stripe Setup](/docs/stripe) and [Integrations](/docs/integrations).

---

## How a payment becomes a payout

Every card payment goes through three stages in Stripe:

1. **Pending.** The customer has paid, and Stripe is settling the money. For US accounts, this usually takes about **2 business days**.
2. **Available.** The money is cleared and ready to send to your bank.
3. **Paid out.** Stripe sends your available balance to your bank. Your bank may take a little longer to show it, and payouts that land on a weekend or holiday arrive the next business day.

New US Stripe accounts start on **daily automatic payouts**. Each day, Stripe sends whatever has become available.

<Callout type="info" title="Your first payout takes longer">
Stripe holds your very first payout for **7 to 14 days** after your first successful live payment (usually 7). Stripe says this wait can't be waived. After that, payouts follow your normal schedule. See Stripe's guide, [Waiting on your first payout](https://support.stripe.com/questions/waiting-on-your-first-stripe-payout-what-you-need-to-know).
</Callout>

### Changing your payout schedule

You change the schedule in Stripe, not in SimplePress. In your Stripe Dashboard, go to **Settings → Payouts** and choose:

- **Daily**
- **Weekly**, on the day you pick
- **Monthly**, on the day you pick
- **Manual**, where nothing is sent until you start a payout yourself in Stripe

You need Administrator access in Stripe to change it. The schedule only controls when available money is sent. It doesn't make money available any sooner. See Stripe's [payout schedules FAQ](https://support.stripe.com/questions/payout-schedules-faq) and [Payouts guide](https://docs.stripe.com/payouts).

## Fees

SimplePress doesn't charge a platform fee or take a cut of your sales.

- **Stripe's processing fee.** Stripe charges a fee on each card payment and takes it before the money reaches your balance. Stripe's standard rate for US domestic cards is 2.9% + 30¢, but your account's rates may differ. See [Stripe's pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing), or check your Stripe Dashboard.
- **Stripe Tax.** If you turn on automatic sales tax, Stripe charges its own fee for Stripe Tax on transactions where you're registered to collect. See [Stripe Tax pricing](https://stripe.com/tax/pricing) and [Tax](/docs/tax).
- **Disputes.** Stripe charges a fee when a customer disputes a payment. See [Disputes](#disputes) below.

To see what you've paid, open the **What Stripe took** card on [Finances](/docs/finances#what-stripe-took). **Processing fees** there is all Stripe's.

## Where to see your money

**Finances** in your admin gives you the summary. Go to **Insights → Finances** in the sidebar. It shows:

- what customers paid, from your orders (**Money in**);
- Stripe's fees, refunds and payouts for the same period (**What Stripe took**);
- your Stripe balance right now (**Available now** and **Pending**); and
- your last five payouts and their status (**Recent Payouts**).

Only Owners and Managers can see Finances. See [Finances](/docs/finances).

**Your Stripe Dashboard** has the full detail: every payment, every payout, reports and exports. To open it, go to **Settings → Integrations** and click **Open Dashboard** in the Stripe Connect card. It opens in a new tab.

![The Stripe Connect card showing Connected, the Account ID, Open Dashboard and Disconnect buttons, and the Automatic Tax Collection switch](/images/integrations/stripe-connected.png)

*Open Dashboard takes you to your Stripe account.*

## Refunds

You refund card orders from the order page in SimplePress. Click **Issue Refund** in the order's **Payment** card. The money goes back to the customer's card through Stripe, and it comes out of your Stripe balance.

A few things to know:

- For cash, check and other manual orders, **Mark as Refunded** only records the refund. No money moves, so pay the customer back yourself.
- You can't refund an order while its payment is **Disputed**. See [Disputes](#disputes).
- Donation refunds are done in your Stripe Dashboard, not in SimplePress.
- Refunds show in **Refunds issued** under Money in on Finances, and in the **Refunds** tile under What Stripe took.

For the full steps, see [Orders](/docs/orders#refunds).

## Bank debits and other delayed payment methods

Checkout shows shoppers whichever payment methods are switched on in your Stripe account. Cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay and Link are paid on the spot. **Delayed methods** are paid days later: bank debits (ACH Direct Debit, "US bank account"), bank transfers, and voucher methods. These are supported, and you can leave them on.

<Callout type="info" title="Orders from bank payments appear once the money clears">
When a shopper pays by bank, they see an **Order received** page that says their bank payment is processing, and they get a **Your order is processing** email. **No order appears in your Orders list until the money actually clears.** ACH usually takes about 4 business days. Other methods can take longer.
</Callout>

### What happens while it's processing

- **The items are held.** They can't be sold to someone else while the payment clears, for up to 14 days.
- **A discount code is held too.** If the shopper used one, that use is held while the payment clears, so a limited code can't be used up by someone else. If the payment fails, the use is given back and the code works again. See [How Uses Are Counted](/docs/discounts-and-coupons#how-uses-are-counted).
- **You'll only see the payment in Stripe.** In your Stripe Dashboard, open **Payments**. It shows as **Pending** or **Processing**.
- **There's nothing for you to do.** You don't need to create an order or contact the customer.

### When the payment clears

The order appears in your Orders list as **Paid**, like any other order. Stock comes off. The customer gets the usual order confirmation, and you get the usual new-order email.

### If the payment fails

No order is created, and the held items are released. Any discount code the shopper used is given back too. The shopper gets a **Your payment didn't go through** email that invites them to order again. You and your managers get a **Bank payment failed** alert email. You don't need to do anything. See [Emails You Receive](/docs/email-preview#emails-you-receive).

### Good to know

- A method that takes longer than 14 days to settle, such as some bank transfers and vouchers, may release the hold. In rare cases the item could sell out before the order appears.
- Donations on your donate page still take cards only.
- Subscriptions (recurring checkout) work as they always have.
- You can reword the two shopper emails in **Admin → Emails**: **Bank Payment Processing** and **Bank Payment Failed**. See [Notification Emails](/docs/email-preview).

### Choosing which payment methods to offer

If you'd rather only take instant payments, you can switch the delayed ones off in Stripe:

1. Sign in to your [Stripe Dashboard](https://dashboard.stripe.com).
2. Click the **gear icon** (Settings), then open **Payment methods**.
3. Look through the list. Switch off any bank debit, bank transfer or voucher method you don't want. Leave cards and wallets on.

If you don't see the setting, or Stripe has turned something on that you didn't choose, email help@generativejustice.org.

### A customer says they paid but there's no order

1. In your Stripe Dashboard, open **Payments** and find the payment (search by their email or amount).
2. If it shows **Pending** or **Processing**, it's a bank payment that hasn't cleared yet. The order will appear on its own when it clears. Tell the customer to expect it in a few business days.
3. If it shows **Succeeded** and there's still no order after a day, email help@generativejustice.org.
4. For a payment made before bank debits were supported, the order may never appear. If you can fill it, create a [manual order](/docs/orders#creating-a-manual-order) with the same items. Set **Payment** to **Paid** (stock comes off when it's Paid), and tick **Send order confirmation email to customer**. The money is already in your Stripe account. A manual order doesn't take a second payment. If you can't fill the order, refund the payment from the Stripe Dashboard. A refund issued only in Stripe doesn't appear on any order in SimplePress, because there isn't one.

## Other ways to get paid

Checkout goes through Stripe only. You can still take cash, check, Venmo, Cash App, Zelle and more through manual orders, invoices and your donate page. These never go through Stripe, so there's no Stripe fee, and they don't appear in What Stripe took or in your payouts. See [Other ways to get paid](/docs/integrations#other-ways-to-get-paid).

## When Stripe pauses payments

Sometimes Stripe pauses charges on an account. This usually happens when it needs identity or business verification, more documents, or a response to a dispute review. While charges are paused, **checkout fails for every shopper**.

Shoppers don't see a warning up front. Your checkout page loads as normal, and they fill in their details. Only when they click **Continue to Payment** does Stripe refuse to start the payment, and they see the generic message "Failed to create checkout session. Please try again." Nothing on your storefront says that payments are paused, so shoppers may simply assume the store is broken. If fixing it with Stripe will take a while, you can post a [banner](/docs/banner-and-popup) saying orders are on hold, or use [Maintenance Mode](/docs/maintenance-mode).

SimplePress tells you in three ways:

- **A red strip across the top of every admin page** that reads "**Payments are paused.** Stripe has disabled charges on your account, so checkout is failing for every shopper." Click **Fix in Stripe** to see what Stripe needs, or **Details** to open **Settings → Integrations**. Staff don't see the strip, and it can't be dismissed.
- **A Charges paused badge** on the Stripe Connect card in **Settings → Integrations**, with a red alert titled "Stripe has paused charges on this account".
- **An email** with the subject "Action required: [your store] can't accept payments".

![The payments paused email: Your store can't accept payments, with the status Charges disabled — checkout is failing and a Resolve in Stripe button](/images/payments-and-payouts/payments-disabled-email.png)

*The email has a **Resolve in Stripe** button and a link to Settings → Integrations.*

The email goes to the **Owner Email** in **Settings → General** and to every team member with the **Owner** or **Manager** role. Staff don't get it. Each person gets their own copy. See [General Settings](/docs/general-settings#owner-email) and [Team & roles](/docs/team-and-roles). For every email that comes to you, see [Emails You Receive](/docs/email-preview#emails-you-receive). The email is sent once, when charges are first paused. It isn't repeated, and no email is sent when charges come back.

**To fix it,** sign in to Stripe and complete whatever it asks for. Once Stripe turns charges back on, everything clears itself within a few minutes. You don't need to reconnect Stripe or change your store. See [Integrations](/docs/integrations).

## Disputes

### What a dispute is

A dispute, also called a chargeback, is when a customer asks their bank to reverse a card payment. They might not recognise the charge, say the order never arrived, or say it wasn't what they paid for. Stripe takes the disputed amount out of your balance while the bank decides.

### The email you get

When a customer disputes an order, SimplePress emails you with the subject "Payment dispute opened on order #[number]".

![The dispute email: Payment dispute opened, with the order number, disputed amount, reason and respond-by date, and a Respond in Stripe button](/images/payments-and-payouts/dispute-email.png)

*The email shows the order, the amount, the reason and the date you must respond by.*

The email shows:

- the **Order** number;
- the **Disputed amount**;
- the **Reason**, in Stripe's own words (for example `fraudulent` or `product_not_received`);
- **Respond by**, the deadline, when Stripe gives one; and
- a **Respond in Stripe** button that opens your disputes in Stripe.

Like the payments paused email, it goes to the **Owner Email** in **Settings → General** and to every Owner and Manager on your team, each in their own copy. Stripe also emails the owner of your Stripe account. See [Emails You Receive](/docs/email-preview#emails-you-receive) for all of your alert emails in one place.

### What happens to the order

- The order's payment changes to **Disputed**. The order page shows "Payment: Disputed". The order status itself doesn't change.
- A note is added to the order's internal notes with the amount, the reason and the respond-by date. Staff can't see internal notes.
- While the payment is disputed, the refund button and the fulfillment and **Mark Ready for Pickup** controls are hidden.
- The order stays in **Money in** on Finances at its full value until the dispute closes.
- In the Orders list, the order shows a red **Disputed** badge. To see every disputed order, open **Filters** and set **Payment** to **Disputed**. See [Orders](/docs/orders).
- You can't change the payment status while the dispute is open. In **More Options → Override Status → Payment Status**, the choices are greyed out with the note "dispute open". SimplePress updates the status for you when Stripe closes the dispute.

### How to respond

You respond in Stripe, not in SimplePress.

1. Click **Respond in Stripe** in the email, or open your Stripe Dashboard and go to **Payments → Disputes**.
2. Open the dispute. Choose **Accept dispute** if you agree the customer should get their money back, or **Counter dispute** to send evidence.
3. If you counter, gather everything that backs you up: the order details, tracking and delivery confirmation, messages with the customer, and your refund policy. You can find most of this on the order page in SimplePress.
4. Submit before the deadline. You only get one submission, so include everything at once.

Things to know, from Stripe:

- **The deadline is usually 7 to 21 days**, depending on the card network. If you don't respond in time, **you lose automatically** and can't get the money back.
- **Every dispute costs a $15 fee** (US). You pay it whether you win or lose, and it isn't returned.
- **Countering costs another $15** (US). This one is returned if you win.
- The bank's decision can take **up to three months** after you submit evidence.

See Stripe's guide, [Respond to disputes](https://docs.stripe.com/disputes/responding).

### Won or lost

When Stripe closes the dispute, SimplePress updates the order and adds a note. No email is sent at this point, so check the order or Stripe.

| Outcome | What happens to the order | How it shows on Finances |
| ------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| **Won** | Payment goes back to **Paid**. Note: "Stripe dispute won — $X returned." | The order stays in Money in as normal. In What Stripe took, the money Stripe took and then returned shows under **Dispute adjustments**. If both fall in the range you picked, they cancel out and the tile is hidden. |
| **Lost** | Payment becomes **Refunded**. If the dispute covered the whole order, the order becomes **Refunded** too. Note: "Stripe dispute lost — $X withdrawn." | The amount counts in **Refunds issued** under Money in. The money Stripe kept shows under **Dispute adjustments**. |

Stripe's dispute fees also come out of your Stripe balance, so they show on the What Stripe took card too. If Stripe closes a dispute some other way (for example, an early warning that never became a full dispute), the payment goes back to **Paid**, with a note saying how it closed.

### Tips to prevent disputes

Many disputes happen because a customer doesn't recognise a charge or can't find their order. A few habits help:

- **Use a clear statement descriptor.** This is the name customers see on their bank statement. Make it match your store name. You set it in your Stripe account's business settings. See Stripe's guide, [What is a statement descriptor?](https://support.stripe.com/questions/what-is-a-statement-descriptor-and-how-do-i-update-it)
- **Add tracking numbers** when you ship. Customers get the tracking link by email, and you have proof of delivery if you need it. See [Orders](/docs/orders#fulfilling-and-tracking).
- **Publish clear policies.** Clear refund, return and shipping policies set expectations, and they're useful evidence. See [Pages & Policies](/docs/pages-and-policies#policies).
- **Answer customers quickly.** A customer who gets a quick reply or a refund is less likely to go to their bank.

## What you can and can't do

**What you can do:**

- Take card payments that go straight to your own Stripe account, with no SimplePress fee
- See your balance, fees, refunds and last five payouts on [Finances](/docs/finances)
- Open your Stripe Dashboard from **Settings → Integrations**
- Refund card orders from the order page
- Get an email when Stripe pauses payments or a customer disputes a charge
- Find disputed orders with the **Disputed** payment filter in Orders

**What you can't do:**

- Change your payout schedule or start a payout from SimplePress. Do it in Stripe.
- See more than your last five payouts in SimplePress. Use your Stripe Dashboard.
- Respond to a dispute from SimplePress. You respond in Stripe.
- Refund an order while its payment is disputed
- Override the payment status of a disputed order
- Choose who gets the dispute and payments paused emails. They go to the Owner Email and every Owner and Manager.
- Use a card processor other than Stripe
