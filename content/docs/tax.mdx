## Overview

SimplePress works with **Stripe Tax** to work out the sales tax on each order at checkout. You set Stripe Tax up in your Stripe account first. Then you flip one switch in SimplePress, and tax is added to checkout automatically.

<Callout type="warn" title="We are not tax advisors">

SimplePress is not a tax advisor, accountant, or attorney. Nothing on this page, or anywhere else in the platform, is legal or tax advice. Sales tax rules vary by state and change often. Please talk to a qualified tax professional about whether you need to collect tax, where you need to register, and how to file.

</Callout>

This page covers:

- What "nexus" means and how to know when it applies to you
- The right order for setting up automatic tax
- What gets taxed, and what customers see
- Where the tax you collect shows up for you
- What Stripe Tax does and doesn't do, including what it costs

## Understanding nexus

"Nexus" is the legal term for a connection to a state that makes you responsible for collecting its sales tax. There are two kinds:

- **Physical nexus** means you have a presence in the state, such as an office, a warehouse or employees.
- **Economic nexus** means you sell enough into a state, even with no presence there. The 2018 Supreme Court case *South Dakota v. Wayfair* let states set these limits.

Most states with a sales tax now have an economic nexus rule. Most use a **sales** threshold of $100,000. Some also count transactions, often 200, and a number of states have dropped that count. A few examples:

| State | Threshold |
| --- | --- |
| New York | $500,000 in sales **and** 100 transactions |
| California, Texas | $500,000 in sales, no transaction count |
| Florida | $100,000 in sales, no transaction count |
| Many other states | $100,000 in sales (some also count 200 transactions) |

These numbers are only a rough guide, and the exact window (calendar year or rolling 12 months) differs by state. Check the current rule for each state before you rely on it. Stripe keeps a page for every state: see [how Stripe Tax handles each US state](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/united-states/collect-tax). Stripe also watches your sales for you. In your Stripe Dashboard, **Tax → Locations** flags states where you are close to or over a threshold. Learn more in [Stripe's threshold monitoring guide](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/monitoring). It is a helpful warning, but you still decide whether to register.

As a rule of thumb, register in a state before you cross its threshold or as soon as you do. A tax professional can tell you where you stand today.

## Setting up automatic tax, in the right order

If your store sells products, **Set up sales tax** is a step on your [Setup Guide](/docs/launch-checklist#the-steps). It ticks off when automatic tax is turned on. If you've reviewed your tax setup and decided automatic tax isn't right for you, click **Mark as done** on that step instead. It opens the Tax Guide.

Do these three things in this order. The order matters.

<Callout type="info" title="Stripe first, switch second">

The switch only turns on after Stripe Tax is set up in your Stripe account, so do the Stripe part first. The Tax Guide inside your admin lists the steps in the same order. If you flip the switch too early, SimplePress turns it back off and shows you a message.

</Callout>

### Step 1: Set up Stripe Tax in your Stripe Dashboard

There are two parts to this. Both happen in Stripe, not in SimplePress. See [Set up Stripe Tax](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/set-up) for Stripe's own walkthrough.

**Part A: Confirm your head office address.**

1. Sign in at [dashboard.stripe.com](https://dashboard.stripe.com).
2. Open your Stripe Tax settings at [dashboard.stripe.com/settings/tax](https://dashboard.stripe.com/settings/tax).
3. Review your **head office address**. Stripe usually fills it in from your business address. Confirm it. It has to be a real US street address, not a PO box.
4. Choose a **preset product tax code** that matches what you sell, such as a general code for physical goods. Stripe uses it for products that don't carry their own tax code.

This is the part SimplePress checks. The switch will not turn on until Stripe says your Tax settings are complete.

**Part B: Add a registration for each state where you collect tax.**

1. In the Stripe Dashboard, go to **Tax → Locations**.
2. Click **+Add registration** and pick the state.
3. Choose **I've already registered** and enter the details Stripe asks for, such as your permit number.
4. Choose **Start collecting immediately**, or **Schedule tax collection** if you want it to begin on a future date.
5. Check that the state shows the status **Collecting tax**.

**Photo:** Screenshot of the Stripe Tax Locations page in the Stripe Dashboard showing a state with the status "Collecting tax".

See [Register for sales tax with Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/registering) for the full steps. You need a sales tax permit from the state itself before you can add its registration. You get that from the state's Department of Revenue, not from Stripe.

<Callout type="info" title="No registration yet? Nothing breaks.">

If you turn the switch on before you have registered in any state, checkout still works. Stripe simply charges no tax. That is the right result if you don't owe tax yet. Stripe only collects tax in states where you have a registration, and customers in other states are not charged.

</Callout>

### Step 2: Turn on the switch in SimplePress

1. In your admin, go to **Settings → Integrations**.
2. In the **Stripe Connect** card, find **Automatic Tax Collection**. It only appears once Stripe is connected. See [Integrations](/docs/integrations) if you haven't connected yet.
3. Flip the switch on. It saves right away, and a message says "Automatic tax collection enabled".

![The Automatic Tax Collection switch on Settings, Integrations, with the Set up taxes link in its description](/images/tax/tax-switch.png)

*The switch is off here. Click "Set up taxes →" to open the Tax Guide.*

The text under the switch spells out what it needs: Stripe Tax set up on your connected account with your head office address. It also reminds you that tax is only collected in places where you've added a registration in Stripe.

If something is missing, the switch flips back off and a red message tells you why. These are the messages you can see:

| Message | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Connect a Stripe account before enabling automatic tax collection. | Stripe isn't connected yet. Connect it first. |
| Stripe Tax isn't set up on your account yet. Finish your Tax settings in the Stripe Tax Dashboard, mainly your head office address, then try again. | Stripe says your Tax settings aren't complete. The usual cause is the head office address from Part A. Finish Step 1, then try again. |
| Could not verify your Stripe Tax setup. Make sure your Tax settings, including your head office address, are complete in the Stripe Tax Dashboard, then try again. | SimplePress couldn't reach Stripe to check. Make sure Stripe Tax is set up, wait a moment and try again. |

Each message is followed by an **Open Stripe Tax Dashboard →** link. SimplePress only checks that your Stripe Tax settings are complete. It does not look at your registrations. Adding them is still your job, in Step 1.

While the switch is on, the card also shows a note: "Stripe will collect sales tax only in states where you've added a registration. Customers in other states won't be charged tax." Its **Manage registrations →** link opens your Stripe registrations.

Turning the switch off is never blocked. Do that any time to stop tax at checkout.

### Step 3: Do a test checkout

Place a test order shipping to an address in a state where you have a registration. Check that tax appears on the order total. Then look at the order in **Orders**: it has a Tax row. Also try an address in a state where you are not registered. It should show no tax.

## What gets taxed

| Where | Is tax added? |
| --- | --- |
| Product checkout | Yes, when the switch is on. |
| Subscriptions | Yes, when the switch is on. Each delivery is taxed. |
| Donations | **Never.** A gift isn't a sale, so tax is never added even with the switch on. |
| Invoices | Not by Stripe. Invoices use the **Default tax rate** in **Settings → Invoices**, which is a percentage you type in. See [Invoices](/docs/invoices). |
| Manual orders | Not calculated for you. The order form has a **Tax** field where you type the amount. See [Orders](/docs/orders). |

## What customers see

Customers see the tax as its own line on their order total, between shipping and the final total. It also appears on their receipt and confirmation email, and on their order status page.

![The customer order status page showing Subtotal, Shipping, Tax and Total](/images/tax/order-status-tax.png)

*The order status page. The same Tax line shows on your order page, the printed order and the emails.*

On a subscription's subscribe form with the switch on, the Tax row says **Calculated at checkout**. The total reads **Per-delivery total (before tax)**, because the final tax depends on the shipping address. The same "(before tax)" wording shows on your subscription page in the admin.

## Where collected tax shows for you

Tax you collect is not your money. You are holding it for the state until you file. Two places in the admin help you keep track. Both are on the **Finances** page, which is covered on [Finances](/docs/finances):

- **Money in** has a **Sales tax collected** row.
- **Set aside** shows how much tax you collected in the range you picked and for the calendar year so far, so you can keep it separate from your own income.

If the switch is off, the Set aside card shows an alert, "Automatic tax is off", with links to **Settings → Integrations** (where the switch is) and to the Tax Guide.

## What Stripe Tax does and doesn't do

**Stripe Tax handles:**

- Working out the right tax for each checkout from the customer's address and your registrations
- Adding that tax to the order total
- Reports of the tax collected in each location, in your Stripe Dashboard

**Stripe Tax does not, by default:**

- Register you with a state. You get your permit from the state, then add it in Stripe. Stripe also sells a "register for me" service on its higher plan.
- File your sales tax returns or send the money to the state.

### What Stripe Tax costs

Stripe Tax is a Stripe service with its own fees, and SimplePress takes no cut of it. As of this writing, pay-as-you-go pricing is about **0.5% of each transaction** in a state where you are registered to collect. There is also a monthly plan, **Tax Complete**, that starts around $90 a month and includes more services. Stripe's prices change, so check [Stripe Tax pricing](https://stripe.com/tax/pricing) for what applies to your account.

### Filing your returns

You are still responsible for filing and paying. These are your options:

- **File through Stripe.** Stripe offers automated filing for US states with a sales tax. It runs through a partner inside your Stripe Dashboard and needs a paid plan. Stripe also lists other filing partners. See [Stripe's tax filing guide](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/filing) for what's available today.
- **File yourself.** Use the Stripe Tax reports to fill in each state's return.
- **Hire a professional.** An accountant or tax pro can file for you, and can tell you which option fits.

## Adding registrations as you grow

When your sales into a new state get close to its threshold:

1. **Watch your sales by state.** Check **Tax → Locations** in your Stripe Dashboard, or use one of the trackers below.
2. **Register with the state** for a sales tax permit, through its Department of Revenue.
3. **Add the registration in Stripe.** Go to **Tax → Locations → +Add registration**, as in Step 1 Part B.
4. **Nothing changes in SimplePress.** Once the state shows **Collecting tax**, Stripe starts adding tax for customers there.

If you stop selling in a state, you can end its registration in Stripe. That ends the registration in Stripe only. It does not close your account with the state, so ask your tax professional what to do there.

## The INFORM Consumers Act

This is a separate federal law, not a sales tax rule. It asks online marketplaces to collect and check information about sellers who sell a lot. The Finances page has a card that warns you early as your sales grow, and it points to the step in Stripe that finishes your seller details. See [Finances](/docs/finances) for how that card works.

---

## What you can and can't do

**You can:**

- Turn Automatic Tax Collection on or off from **Settings → Integrations**
- Add, change or end registrations in your Stripe Dashboard at any time
- Type a tax amount on a manual order
- Set a default tax rate for invoices
- See tax collected on the Finances page and on each order

**You can't:**

- Turn the switch on before Stripe is connected and Stripe Tax settings are complete
- Have tax added to donations
- File returns or pay the state from inside SimplePress
- Get tax calculated for you on manual orders or invoices
